How reliable is a radial distribution feeder? A six-load-point system, modelled two ways.

This dashboard evaluates the reliability of a Radial Distribution System (RDS) using the standard failure-mode-and-effects method, then checks it against a Monte Carlo simulation of 20,000 years of operation. Every chart is computed from the model behind this page.

Data: synthetic sample feeder with textbook failure rates · replace with utility outage records for real studies.

The feeder

A six-section main feeder. Each node supplies one load point through a fused lateral and a transformer. Any main-feeder fault trips the substation breaker; a lateral or transformer fault is cleared by its own fuse.

Failure rate λ in failures/year (length × rate per km), mean repair time r in hours.

Reliability at each load point

Because the feeder is radial, a customer is exposed to every main-section fault upstream and downstream of it, plus its own lateral and transformer.

What would improve reliability?

Two common upgrades: a normally-open tie switch to a backup feeder, and remote-controlled sectionalizers that cut manual switching time.
